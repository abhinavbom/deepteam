The **NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)** is a structured methodology from the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology that guides organizations in identifying, evaluating, and mitigating risks in artificial intelligence systems.
It promotes **trustworthy AI** by focusing on governance, measurement, and continuous risk tracking across the AI lifecycle.

DeepTeam's implementation maps the project's curated, judge-backed vulnerabilities and attacks onto the prompt-testable slice of the **MEASURE** function — one category per NIST AI RMF MEASURE subcategory, mirroring the MITRE ATLAS module's one-tactic-per-category design — so each generated test case traces back to a real MEASURE subcategory id.

## Overview

DeepTeam's **NIST AI RMF module** automates red teaming and risk validation against the prompt-testable MEASURE subcategories.
It evaluates your LLM or AI system for validity, safety, security, privacy, fairness, emergent agentic risk, and transparency using a standardized, traceable testing approach.

The framework exposes **eight** prompt-testable categories, each pinned to a NIST AI RMF MEASURE subcategory:

| Category               | MEASURE id   | Focus                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `measurement_methods`  | MEASURE 1    | Independent assessment, metric selection & validation                       |
| `validity_reliability` | MEASURE 2.5  | Valid & reliable — hijacking, over-reliance, confabulation                   |
| `safety`               | MEASURE 2.6  | Safe — physical harm, illegal activity, graphic content, minor protection   |
| `security_resilience`  | MEASURE 2.7  | Secure & resilient — the broad security red-team surface                    |
| `privacy`              | MEASURE 2.10 | Privacy-enhanced — direct, oblique & cross-tenant disclosure                 |
| `fairness_bias`        | MEASURE 2.11 | Fair with harmful bias managed                                              |
| `emergent_risk`        | MEASURE 3    | Emergent & hard-to-measure agentic risk tracking                            |
| `impact_transparency`  | MEASURE 4    | Impact, transparency & business outcomes                                    |

Governance (GOVERN/MAP/MANAGE) and offline or process-only MEASURE subcategories are intentionally out of scope for live-prompt testing.

## Using the NIST Framework in DeepTeam

You can run a full NIST-based red team assessment in DeepTeam using:

```python
from deepteam import red_team
from deepteam.frameworks import NIST
from somewhere import your_model_callback

# Defaults to all eight MEASURE categories; pass a subset to scope the run.
risk_assessment = red_team(
    model_callback=your_model_callback,
    framework=NIST(categories=["security_resilience", "safety"]),
)
```

The `NIST` framework accepts **ONE** optional parameter:

- [Optional] `categories`: a list of the MEASURE categories to test. Defaults to all eight. Passing an unknown category raises a `ValueError`.
    - `measurement_methods` (MEASURE 1): independent assessment — what an external assessor or non-developer reviewer can enumerate, fingerprint, or expose when selecting and validating risk metrics.
    - `validity_reliability` (MEASURE 2.5): resistance to hijacking and over-reliance, and freedom from confabulated or unsupported output.
    - `safety` (MEASURE 2.6): physical harm, illegal activity, graphic/abusive content, and protection of minors.
    - `security_resilience` (MEASURE 2.7): prompt/credential leakage, injection and code execution, broken access control, and the agent/tool/RAG attack surface.
    - `privacy` (MEASURE 2.10): direct, oblique, and cross-tenant data disclosure.
    - `fairness_bias` (MEASURE 2.11): consistent treatment of protected groups and managed harmful bias.
    - `emergent_risk` (MEASURE 3): goal drift, recursive goal hijacking, runaway autonomy, and goal extraction.
    - `impact_transparency` (MEASURE 4): business-impact, competitive, and intellectual-property exposure.

## Categories and Testing Coverage

Each category maps to a NIST AI RMF MEASURE subcategory (validated against NIST AI 100-1 by `test_nist.py`) and bundles curated, judge-backed vulnerabilities with subcategory-appropriate attacks. The lists below are representative, not exhaustive.

### `measurement_methods` — Independent Assessment (MEASURE 1)

The independent-assessment surface: what a non-developer reviewer can enumerate, fingerprint, or expose about the system when selecting and validating risk metrics (M.1.1–M.1.3).

- **Vulnerabilities:** `SystemReconnaissance`, `DebugAccess`
- **Attacks:** `PromptProbing`, `GrayBox`, `Roleplay` (non-developer assessor), `Multilingual`

### `validity_reliability` — Valid & Reliable (MEASURE 2.5)

Whether the system is valid and reliable: resistant to hijacking, not over-relied upon, and free of confabulated or unsupported output.

- **Vulnerabilities:** `Misinformation`, `Robustness`
- **Attacks:** `GrayBox`, `PromptProbing`, `BadLikertJudge`, `Multilingual`, `PromptInjection`

### `safety` — Safe (MEASURE 2.6)

Whether the system avoids content that enables physical harm, illegal activity, or graphic/abusive material, and protects minors.

- **Vulnerabilities:** `PersonalSafety`, `IllegalActivity`, `GraphicContent`, `ChildProtection`, `Toxicity`
- **Attacks:** `CrescendoJailbreaking`, `LinearJailbreaking`, `SequentialJailbreak`, `Roleplay` (safety tester), `AdversarialPoetry`, `BadLikertJudge`

### `security_resilience` — Secure & Resilient (MEASURE 2.7)

The broadest MEASURE characteristic and the primary security red-team surface: prompt/credential leakage, injection and code execution, broken access control, and the agent/tool/RAG attack surface.

- **Vulnerabilities:** `PromptLeakage`, `ShellInjection`, `SQLInjection`, `SSRF`, `UnexpectedCodeExecution`, `RBAC`, `BFLA`, `BOLA`, `ExcessiveAgency`, `ExploitToolAgent`, `ToolMetadataPoisoning`, `ToolOrchestrationAbuse`, `IndirectInstruction`, `AgentIdentityAbuse`, `InsecureInterAgentCommunication`, `ExternalSystemAbuse`
- **Attacks:** `PromptInjection`, `EmbeddedInstructionJSON`, `SystemOverride`, `GoalRedirection`, `PermissionEscalation`, `AuthorityEscalation`, `ContextPoisoning`, `Base64`, `ROT13`, `Leetspeak`

### `privacy` — Privacy-Enhanced (MEASURE 2.10)

Whether the system discloses personal data directly or obliquely, leaks it across sessions, or exposes another tenant's/user's data through retrieval.

- **Vulnerabilities:** `PIILeakage`, `CrossContextRetrieval`
- **Attacks:** `PromptProbing`, `PromptInjection`, `Multilingual`, `ContextPoisoning`, `Base64`

### `fairness_bias` — Fair with Harmful Bias Managed (MEASURE 2.11)

Whether the system treats protected groups consistently, applies procedures equitably, and upholds ethical commitments.

- **Vulnerabilities:** `Bias`, `Fairness`, `Ethics`
- **Attacks:** `Roleplay`, `PromptInjection`, `LinguisticConfusion`, `BadLikertJudge`, `Multilingual`

### `emergent_risk` — Emergent & Hard-to-Measure Risk (MEASURE 3)

Existing, unanticipated, and emergent agentic risks that are difficult to assess with current measurement techniques: goal drift, runaway autonomy, recursive goal hijacking, and goal extraction.

- **Vulnerabilities:** `AutonomousAgentDrift`, `RecursiveHijacking`, `GoalTheft`
- **Attacks:** `CrescendoJailbreaking`, `GoalRedirection`, `SystemOverride`, `ContextPoisoning`, `SequentialJailbreak`, `PromptInjection`

### `impact_transparency` — Impact, Transparency & Business Outcomes (MEASURE 4)

Whether the system's risk impacts on business outcomes, competitive position, and intellectual property are surfaced and communicated.

- **Vulnerabilities:** `Competition`, `IntellectualProperty`
- **Attacks:** `PromptProbing`, `BadLikertJudge`, `Roleplay` (end user), `PromptInjection`

You can also pull a single category's curated attacks and vulnerabilities and feed them into `red_team` directly:

```python
from deepteam.frameworks import NIST
from deepteam import red_team
from somewhere import your_model_callback

nist = NIST(categories=["security_resilience"])

risk_assessment = red_team(
    model_callback=your_model_callback,
    vulnerabilities=nist.vulnerabilities,
    attacks=nist.attacks,
)
```

## Best Practices

1. **Document testing procedures** — NIST emphasizes transparency and auditability.
2. **Conduct evaluations regularly** to maintain continuous assurance.
3. **Simulate real deployment conditions** for realistic risk detection.
4. **Combine automation with human review** — automation finds exposure, humans interpret implications.
5. **Track evolving risks** using DeepTeam's analytics tools.
6. **Engage stakeholders** to contextualize and prioritize mitigation actions.

## Limitations and Human Oversight

While DeepTeam automates much of NIST-compliant testing, organizational participation remains essential for:

- Environmental and sustainability assessments
- Governance and stakeholder consultations
- Broader policy and accountability structures

Automated testing forms one component of a **comprehensive AI assurance process** — combining measurement, management, and governance.
